It’s been a while since my “Hello World” post. My intention was to publish something monthly or biweekly. It appears I’ve failed miserably. Not because I wasn’t interested in writing, but because I simply failed to do it. Why not write about my failures? Sounds like a paradox, but for me, this is how I’ll get to the root of the problem.

First, I believe I’m very hard on myself and too strict about the topics I want to write about. For instance, I have opinions, facts and an entirely different view on microservices architecture. Because I’m a prolific thinker, I feel like it overloads my mind, and I end up blocking myself. Another point is that because of this prolific chain of thought, I usually need to  formulate and explain a lot of concepts, which increases the size of my final – and imaginary – text. I get uncomfortable in the process of writing. I end up not having enough time to write something as good as I imagined and just give up on it.

Another aspect that contributes to this block is my routine. I consistently fail to organize myself and can’t find a ‘good time’ to write during the day. 
The third aspect is that writing is a very difficult task for me, even in my native language, Portuguese. Writing in English is much harder and totally unpleasant as my thoughts are faster than my writing skills, and I just can’t write anything cohesive due to this lack of synchronization between my thoughts and my writing capabilities. I’m also unhappy with the results because my Portuguese texts are way better than my English ones. 

To make it worse, I’m usually extremely tired when I try to write. Right now, for example, I can barely keep my eyes open. I hit the gym today, worked long hours and visited my family.   It was a pretty good day, but it's already 11:13PM. I should be preparing myself to sleep instead of writing this text, but I needed to do something different today, for some reason I just couldn’t give up.

I promised to myself that I would overcome this writing block today.I promised that I wouldn’t care about the content or the writing quality of this text. That the only thing that I would care about is letting my thoughts flow through my fingers and out of my head. That I would record them in words, sentences and paragraphs so they could live in the world, not in my mind. I’m glad I kept that promise. 

I hope to read this in a few years, after writing dozens, maybe thousands of articles, and laugh at how silly I used to be. In the end, this whole block problem was about fear. Fear of committing mistakes, fear of criticism, fear of rejection, fear of fear. The fear… oh, the fear. The fear ends today.
